# Due Diligence Procedures

## Introduction

This guide provides step-by-step instructions for implementing a compliant due diligence system under EUDR. A robust due diligence system is the foundation of EUDR compliance and must address all stages of the supply chain.

## Phase 1: Supply Chain Mapping

### Step 1.1: Identify Supply Chain Participants

**Objective**: Create complete map of all entities in supply chain from production to import.

**Actions**:
- List all direct suppliers
- Document supplier locations and contacts
- For each supplier, identify their suppliers (traceability)
- Continue mapping to producer level
- Document all intermediaries (traders, processors, transporters)

**Documentation**:
- Supplier registry with contact details
- Organizational structure diagrams
- Entity relationship maps
- Update frequency: quarterly minimum

### Step 1.2: Map Commodity Flows

**Objective**: Track how commodities move through supply chain.

**Actions**:
- Document commodity types and quantities
- Track origins (country, region, plot level if possible)
- Record processing and transformation steps
- Identify consolidation points (where supplies merge)
- Note storage and transportation routes

**Documentation**:
- Flow diagrams showing commodity movement
- Batch/lot tracking systems
- Processing documentation
- Container and shipment records

### Step 1.3: Document Geographic Origins

**Objective**: Establish geographic origin at production level.

**Actions**:
- Collect country of production information
- Request regional information where available
- Obtain plot-level location for high-risk regions
- Verify origin with producer documentation
- Maintain GPS coordinates if possible

**Documentation**:
- Country codes and regional identifiers
- GPS coordinates or property identifiers
- Producer certificates of origin
- Land registry documentation

## Phase 2: Risk Assessment

### Step 2.1: Gather Risk Intelligence

**Objective**: Collect information about deforestation risks.

**Actions**:
- Research historical deforestation rates by country
- Review EUDR country risk classifications
- Check regulatory enforcement capacity
- Assess governance quality
- Review environmental certifications

**Information Sources**:
- EUDR official country assessments
- Global Forest Watch data
- FSC/PEFC certifications
- Government forestry databases
- Industry certifications (Rainforest Alliance, etc.)

### Step 2.2: Assess Risk Factors

**Objective**: Evaluate deforestation risk for each supplier.

**Risk Categories**:

**Geographic Risk**:
- Country deforestation rate
- Regional legal deforestation limits
- Governance effectiveness
- Enforcement history

**Commodity Risk**:
- Commodity type (cattle, soy, palm oil, etc.)
- Known deforestation association
- Production methods
- Market competition factors

**Operational Risk**:
- Supplier transparency level
- Financial stability
- Previous violations
- Certification status

### Step 2.3: Assign Risk Levels

**Objective**: Classify suppliers by deforestation risk.

**Risk Classifications**:

**Low Risk**:
- Country <1% deforestation rate
- Certified sustainable sources
- Strong governance
- Transparent operations

**Medium Risk**:
- Country 1-4% deforestation rate
- Some certifications present
- Moderate governance
- Some documentation gaps

**High Risk**:
- Country >4% deforestation rate
- Weak governance/enforcement
- Minimal certifications
- Recent non-compliance

## Phase 3: Mitigation Measures

### Step 3.1: Design Mitigation Strategy

**Objective**: Develop actions to address identified risks.

**Mitigation Approach**:

**Low-Risk Suppliers**:
- Standard documentation requirements
- Annual verification
- Periodic audits
- Relationship monitoring

**Medium-Risk Suppliers**:
- Enhanced documentation requirements
- Quarterly compliance checks
- More frequent audits
- Capacity building support

**High-Risk Suppliers**:
- Detailed contracts with compliance clauses
- Monthly or real-time monitoring
- On-site audits
- Third-party verification required
- Consider supplier replacement

### Step 3.2: Implement Contractual Measures

**Objective**: Establish contractual commitments to EUDR compliance.

**Contract Requirements**:
- Compliance with EUDR obligations
- Documentation provision rights
- Right to audit and inspect
- Penalties for non-disclosure
- Termination rights for violations
- Warranty of legal compliance
- Data sharing permissions

### Step 3.3: Establish Monitoring Systems

**Objective**: Create ongoing monitoring capability.

**Monitoring Elements**:
- Quarterly documentation updates
- Periodic supplier certifications
- Technology systems for tracking
- Alert mechanisms for high-risk periods
- Regular review of satellite data
- Third-party verification where needed

## Phase 4: Documentation and Evidence

### Step 4.1: Gather Supporting Documentation

**Objective**: Collect proof of due diligence completion.

**Required Documentation**:
- **Supplier contracts** with compliance clauses
- **Certificates of origin** from producers
- **Land use documentation** (permits, registrations)
- **Audit reports** from certifications
- **Test reports** (lab analysis if required)
- **Import/export paperwork**
- **Processing documentation** showing commodity transformation

### Step 4.2: Organize Documentation System

**Objective**: Maintain organized, accessible records.

**System Requirements**:
- Centralized document repository
- Clear filing structure
- Version control
- Digital storage with backups
- Retention for 5+ years
- Accessibility for audits

### Step 4.3: Prepare Due Diligence Statement

**Objective**: Compile comprehensive compliance statement.

**Statement Contents**:
- Executive summary of supply chain
- Risk assessment findings
- Mitigation measures implemented
- Monitoring systems in place
- Documentation references
- Responsible officer signature
- Annual update mechanism

## Phase 5: Verification and Audit

### Step 5.1: Internal Verification

**Objective**: Verify system effectiveness internally.

**Internal Audit Activities**:
- Document completeness check
- Risk assessment validation
- Supplier compliance verification
- Process effectiveness review
- Compliance metric tracking

**Frequency**: Quarterly minimum

### Step 5.2: External Verification

**Objective**: Obtain third-party confirmation.

**Third-Party Activities**:
- Certification body audits (FSC, PEFC, etc.)
- Compliance auditors
- Traceability verification
- Site inspections
- Document verification

**Frequency**: Annually minimum

## Best Practices

### Documentation Management
- Use digital tools for tracking
- Implement document version control
- Maintain audit trails
- Regular backup procedures
- Train staff on procedures

### Communication
- Clear supplier communication
- Regular compliance meetings
- Transparent expectations
- Training support
- Feedback mechanisms

### Continuous Improvement
- Monitor effectiveness metrics
- Adjust risk assessments
- Update mitigation measures
- Respond to new intelligence
- Incorporate lessons learned

## Common Challenges and Solutions

### Challenge: Lack of Supplier Transparency
**Solution**: Include in contracts, provide support, consider replacement

### Challenge: Complex Supply Chains
**Solution**: Use technology tools, engage intermediaries, segment by risk

### Challenge: Cost of Compliance
**Solution**: Streamline processes, leverage certifications, budget appropriately

### Challenge: Changing Deforestation Data
**Solution**: Implement quarterly reviews, set alert thresholds, maintain flexibility
