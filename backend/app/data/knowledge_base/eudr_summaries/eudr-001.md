# EUDR Regulation Overview

## Introduction

The EU Deforestation Regulation (EUDR) is a landmark regulation adopted by the European Union to address global deforestation and forest degradation. It establishes requirements for businesses placing commodities and products associated with deforestation on the EU market.

## Key Objectives

1. **Halt Deforestation**: Prevent further forest loss in supply chains
2. **Protect Biodiversity**: Safeguard natural ecosystems and biodiversity
3. **Ensure Traceability**: Establish clear supply chain documentation requirements
4. **Hold Companies Accountable**: Establish legal liability for non-compliance

## Scope of Application

The regulation applies to:
- Natural and legal persons
- EU-based and third-country operators
- Placement of commodities and products on the EU market
- Covered commodities: cattle, cocoa, coffee, palm oil, soy, wood, rubber

## Core Requirements

### Due Diligence Obligations
- Identify and assess deforestation risks
- Implement mitigation measures
- Maintain documentation and traceability records
- Report and communicate on compliance efforts

### Traceability Requirements
- Map supply chains to country of production
- Verify proof of origin and legality
- Maintain auditable records
- Enable tracking of commodities to land origin

### Timeline

| Milestone | Date |
|-----------|------|
| Regulation Adopted | December 2022 |
| Enforcement Begins | December 2024 |
| Full Compliance | December 2025 |

## Penalties for Non-Compliance

- Administrative fines up to 4% of turnover
- Product seizure and destruction
- Market access bans
- Criminal liability for deliberate violations
