# EUDR Enforcement Mechanisms

## Regulatory Authority and Oversight

### Competent Authorities

Each EU Member State designates authorities responsible for:
- Monitoring market compliance
- Investigating non-compliance
- Imposing administrative penalties
- Cooperating internationally

### Coordination Structure

- **Central contact points**: Member States coordinate enforcement
- **Information sharing**: Database of enforcement actions
- **Peer reviews**: Assessment of enforcement effectiveness
- **Cross-border cooperation**: Unified approach to market oversight

## Market Surveillance and Inspection

### Inspection Powers

Competent authorities may:
- Inspect premises and records
- Request documentation on demand
- Take samples for testing
- Conduct surprise inspections
- Access digital systems and databases

### Inspection Triggers

Authorities may inspect based on:
- **Risk assessment**: High-risk operators or commodities
- **Complaints**: Third-party allegations
- **Statistical sampling**: Routine monitoring
- **International alerts**: EUDR violations in other markets

## Administrative Penalties

### Penalty Structure

**Administrative fines** for non-compliance:
- Up to 4% of annual EU turnover
- Applied per violation or continuously
- Adjusted based on severity and duration

### Penalty Determination Factors

- Gravity and duration of violation
- Intentional vs. negligent conduct
- Company size and financial capacity
- Cooperation with authorities
- Previous violations

### Types of Violations Penalized

- Failure to establish due diligence system
- Incomplete or false documentation
- Lack of traceability records
- Non-compliance with benchmarking
- Failure to respond to requests

## Product Seizure and Destruction

### Authority Powers

Competent authorities may:
- **Seize** non-compliant products
- **Destroy** products that cannot be remedied
- **Prohibit sale** of affected items
- **Recall** products already distributed

### Conditions for Seizure

Products may be seized if:
- Lacking proper due diligence documentation
- From non-registered operators
- From countries with elevated risk
- Presenting deforestation risk

### Destruction and Disposal

- Destruction at importer's expense
- Alternative use only if approved
- Documentation of destruction process
- Recovery of costs from responsible parties

## Trading Suspension and Bans

### Operator Suspension

Competent authorities may suspend:
- **Import permissions** for non-compliant operators
- **Commercial licenses** for violations
- **Trading rights** pending corrective action
- **Exemptions** if conditions no longer met

### Grounds for Suspension

- Repeated non-compliance
- Failure to remediate violations
- Intentional deception
- Risk of continued violations

### Duration and Lifting

- Suspension effective immediately
- Duration based on violation severity
- Suspension lifted upon compliance demonstration
- May be temporary or permanent

## Criminal Liability

### Intentional Violations

Criminal penalties apply for:
- Deliberate deforestation facilitation
- Intentional false documentation
- Systematic violation concealment
- Fraudulent claims

### Criminal Sanctions

- Imprisonment (member states determine duration)
- Criminal fines
- Disqualification from business
- Asset seizure

### Prosecution Requirements

- Intentional conduct required (negligence insufficient)
- Significant harm or risk threshold
- Member state discretion on prosecution
- International cooperation for enforcement

## Remediation and Compliance

### Corrective Action Periods

When violations detected:
- Company given opportunity to remediate
- Reasonable timeframe for correction
- Submission of remediation plan
- Verification of corrective measures

### Compliance Verification

- Companies must demonstrate full compliance
- Documentation submission required
- Inspection to verify compliance
- Ongoing monitoring post-remediation

## Monitoring and Reporting

### Competent Authority Reporting

Authorities must report:
- **Violations detected**: Number and nature
- **Penalties imposed**: Amounts and recipients
- **Products seized**: Quantity and value
- **Enforcement actions**: Suspension, bans, etc.

### Annual Coordination

- Member States share enforcement data
- Commission publishes aggregated statistics
- Assessment of enforcement effectiveness
- Recommendations for improvements

## Third-Party Rights and Appeals

### Standing to Challenge

- Affected companies may appeal decisions
- NGOs may report violations
- Consumers can lodge complaints
- Operators entitled to fair process

### Appeals Process

- Administrative appeals to authorities
- Judicial review available
- European Court jurisdiction
- Interim measures possible

## Mutual Assistance and International Cooperation

### Cross-Border Enforcement

- EU member states cooperate
- Information sharing on violators
- Joint investigation capabilities
- Coordinated enforcement actions

### Third-Country Cooperation

- EUDR data shared with producer countries
- Capacity building support
- Verification assistance
- Enforcement coordination where possible
